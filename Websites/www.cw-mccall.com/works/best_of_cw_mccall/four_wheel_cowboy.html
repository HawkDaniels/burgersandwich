Q. What’s wrong with this song?

A. Nothing; it’s just a really good case of artistic license.

If you attempt to follow this journey on the map, you’ll note that the locations are mentioned in the order

  1. Denver
  2. Three hundred miles to Santa Fe
  3. Trinidad
  4. Walsenburg
  5. Two hundred miles to Santa Fe
  6. Raton Pass
  7. Glorieta Hill
  8. Pecos
  9. Fifteen miles to Santa Fe
  10. One mile to Santa Fe

If you visited those locations in the order that they were mentioned, you’d be backtracking a couple of times.

  1. Denver. No problem here; this is the starting point.
  2. Three hundred miles to Santa Fe. So far, so good. This point would be near Pueblo, Colorado.
  3. Trinidad. This is okay; Trindad’s south of Pueblo, and about one hundred ninety miles from Santa Fe.
  4. Walsenburg? Walsenburg is forty miles north of Trinidad; we’ve already driven through it.
  5. Two hundred miles to Santa Fe. Which would put us between Walsenburg and Trinidad.
  6. Raton Pass. It’s on the Colorado/New Mexico border, ten miles south of Trinidad.
  7. Glorieta Hill. It has nothing to do with Raton Pass. Glorieta is west of Pecos, which is about twenty miles southeast of Santa Fe. And about one hundred sixy miles from Raton Pass.
  8. Pecos. It’s east of Glorieta.
  9. Fifteen miles to Santa Fe. We’re just west of Glorieta, here.
  10. One mile to Santa Fe. Just south of our destination.

Strangely, C.W. never actually arrives in Santa Fe.

Okay, so I’m nitpicking. Enjoy the trip, anyway.

“Four Wheel Cowboy” can be found on the album The Best of C.W. McCall.
